# WAiFF Master Outreach Template v2 (team and LLM version)

**How to use this document**
- **In an LLM chat** (DeepSeek, Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT): paste this whole file first, then paste one filled RESEARCH BLOCK (section 6) per company. The model returns the email, the follow-ups and the LinkedIn texts in the output format in section 9.
- **For the team:** sections 1 to 5 are the context every person and every model must know. Section 6 is what the researcher fills in. Sections 7 to 10 are the writing rules.
- **Last updated:** 24 Sep 2026. It is built from an audit of about 990 emails sent in September, and the rules come from what actually got replies.

---

## 1. Who we are and what we sell (the model's context)

**Sender.** Aditya Upadhyay, India Ambassador, World AI Film Festival (WAiFF). All mail goes from adiup394@gmail.com, because there is no WAiFF work email. Every email is written as Aditya, in his voice.

**The festival.** The name is always **World AI Film Festival (WAiFF)**.
- Never "World AI International Film Festival", "WAFF" or "WAIFF India Festival".
- It is **not** the Cannes Film Festival. Always say "the Palais des Festivals in Cannes".
- It is the international festival for films made with AI.
- 2025 (1st edition) was in Nice. 2026 (2nd edition) was the first at the Palais des Festivals in Cannes. The **third edition is the Grand Finale on 6 and 7 April 2027 at the Palais**.
- About a dozen national editions ("Road to Cannes") feed the Grand Finale. They include São Paulo, Seoul and Kyoto (2026), Buenos Aires (30 Oct 2026), Istanbul (4 Nov 2026), London (24 Nov 2026), Los Angeles, a ten-country Africa franchise (Abidjan) and China.

**We sell two things, and never both in one email.**

1. **SPONSOR (about 80% of daily outreach).**
   - **What:** sponsorship of WAiFF as a whole. That means the Cannes Grand Finale and any national editions the sponsor wants.
   - **Size:** packages run from about $100,000 to $450,000+.
   - **Never write a price in a cold email.**
   - **What a sponsor gets:**
     - A prize or competition named after the brand, presented on stage in Cannes. CapCut and MiniMax did this in 2026, and CapCut's name moves to a whole competition in 2027.
     - Its product in the hands of thousands of AI filmmakers across the editions.
     - Demo space, workshops, masterclasses and hackathons.
     - Speaking slots at the Pro Talks.
     - Film-festival prestige and press (red carpet at the Palais, Gong Li as 2026 President, Screen International and Variety coverage).
     - Visibility in every edition city.
2. **ORGANIZER (about 20%).**
   - **What:** a licence for one big Indian company to own and run WAiFF India in December 2026.
   - **What the licensee keeps:** local revenue from submission fees, tickets, its own local sponsorship packages, hackathons and broadcast.
   - **Cannes route:** India puts one film forward for the WAIFF Horizons competition at the Grand Finale.
   - **Existing licensees:** TOKYO EPIC runs Kyoto, Vision Culture ran Seoul with Lotte, Ay Yapim runs Istanbul, and Kaydan Group runs Africa.
   - **Status:** the India edition is **not publicly announced**. Never write "confirmed", a date, or a city.

## 3. Never write these (each one has already cost us replies)

| Never write | Why |
|---|---|
| "World AI International Film Festival", "WAFF", "Cannes Film Festival", "Festival de Cannes", "same venue as the Cannes Film Festival" | Wrong name, or confuses WAiFF with a different event. |
| "6,000+ from 100+ countries", "15+ countries, 30+ cities", "300+ professionals", "5,000+ participants" | These figures are not in WAiFF's official materials. |
| "Third Cannes edition", "Cannes 2025" | 2025 was in Nice. |
| "Jury President Gong Li" | She was festival President. |
| Matthew McConaughey, Ron Howard, James Cameron, Val Kilmer | No WAiFF source links them to the festival. |
| Air France, TF1, ARTE, Station F, Banijay or Google "as sponsors" | Not shown in WAiFF documents. |
| "World's first / largest AI film festival" | Not true. |
| "India edition confirmed / launching", "15 December", "Mumbai", "founding sponsor group", "[Company]'s India edition" | The edition is not public. The last one was a merge bug that went to about 54 companies. |
| Any price, tier, package, "media value", "ROI", "1B+ visibility", impressions | Numbers in a cold email get the pitch rejected. |
| Canva or deck links, attachments, Google redirect links (google.com/url?q=...) | Spam and phishing signals. Nobody opens them. |
| "Marco Landi brought back / worked with Steve Jobs", "Apple Global", "Ginésy co-founded" | Overclaims. |
| "Local winners go to Cannes" | Forbidden by the licence. Use the WAIFF Horizons wording. |
| Hype words: "Hope you're doing well", "I hope this email finds you well", "Greetings of the day", "truly remarkable", "journey", "cutting-edge", "Mecca", "movement", "Lumière moment", "hard to buy once things settle", "moving fast", "before it's fully allocated" | They read as AI-written or as pressure. |
| Em dashes, semicolons, exclamation marks, ALL CAPS, emojis | Em dashes alone were in 72% of our subjects. |

## 4. Who we write to

**Sponsor companies must pass all four tests:**
1. **Tech-led or creator-facing.** Software or hardware, or something AI filmmakers and creators use. The niches are open, and section 8 has the idea menu.
2. **Real money.** Public, a unicorn, or Series B or later.
3. **Proof they already pay for big events**, with a link from the last 2 years.
4. **A reason to want AI filmmakers, creators or a Cannes stage.**

Replies came almost only from companies whose product touches filmmakers or creators, or that run events.

**Organizer companies:**
- Big Indian groups with event or production muscle.
- A recent reason to want AI, creators, film or an international IP.
- Able to decide and deliver by December.

**The people (TO and BCC):**
- **TO:** one named decision-maker.
  - Sponsor: CMO, VP or head of brand, head of partnerships or sponsorships, head of creator or community marketing, or country MD.
  - Organizer: CEO or MD, founder, head of IP or live events, or strategy head.
- **BCC:** at most 2 more **named** decision-makers at the **same company and same email domain**. Pick them from different teams: brand, partnerships, creator marketing.
- **Never** TO or BCC: press@, media@, pr@, support@, care@, help@, info@, helpdesk@, reservations@, sales@, careers@, investor relations, company secretary, legal, board, an agency, or a personal Gmail, Yahoo or Hotmail address.
- **Verify every address** (Apollo verified or Hunter). Never mail an address that has bounced before.
- The greeting names the TO person only ("Hi Priya,"), never "Hi team".
- **One company gets one body, one thread, one sequence.** Never pitch the same company again under a new subject.
- Note: BCC to several people at one company can read as a mass send. When there is time, a separate short email to the second person 2 to 3 days later performs better. BCC stays the team default for speed.

## 5. Sending rules

**How to send:**
- Weekdays only.
- Arrive 8 to 11am in the recipient's time zone. Use Gmail schedule-send.
- Space emails a few minutes apart. Never send batches in bursts.
- A single Gmail account should stay around 40 to 50 cold emails a day. If the team sends 100 to 200 a day, split them across several team inboxes.
- Keep bounces under 2%.

**Every day:**
- Answer replies before sending anything new.
- Log every send (company, people, subject, date) in the tracker the same day.
- Add every new company to the do-not-repeat list the same day.

## 7. The email body structure

**SUBJECT**
- 2 to 5 words, lowercase, naming them, their product or one concrete asset.
- Examples: "picsart award in cannes", "shot on razr", "orange, the palais, april", "nodwin and waiff india".
- Banned words: opportunity, partnership, sponsorship, collaboration, invitation, exclusive, proposal, "X x WAiFF", em dash, price, "Re:" or "Fwd:" on a new email.
- The subject must match line 1 of the body.

**BODY, sponsor (50 to 90 words, five short paragraphs)**
1. **Hi {TO first name},**
2. **Why you, why now:** HOOK 1 in one sentence, written as a plain observation, not flattery.
3. **The WAiFF fact** that makes the link obvious, in one sentence. Use the Grand Finale date and venue, plus one proof point from section 2. Add the edition city if there is an EDITION MATCH.
4. **"Idea: ..."**: one concrete asset built around their product, in one sentence.
5. **One interest question:** "Worth a look?", "Open to a one-page outline?", or "Is this on your 2027 radar?"

**BODY, organizer (up to 110 words)**
1. **Dear {TO first name},**
2. **Their IP or event in one sentence** (HOOK 1), and why it proves they can run this.
3. **The licence model:** "The World AI Film Festival, founded by Marco Landi (former President and COO of Apple), licenses one company per country to run its national edition. Kyoto, Seoul and Istanbul already work this way. India is still open, for December."
4. **What they keep:** "The licensee owns the edition and keeps its revenue: submissions, tickets, sponsors. India also puts one film forward for the WAIFF Horizons competition at the Grand Finale in Cannes."
5. **Why it fits their business**, in one line.
6. **"Would a 20-minute look be useful?"**

**CLOSE (always the same)**
```
Not relevant? Just say so and I won't follow up.

Aditya Upadhyay
India Ambassador, World AI Film Festival
worldaifilmfestival.com
+91 {mobile} (WhatsApp)
```

Type the website as plain text. Never paste it from a Gmail window, because that produces a google.com/url redirect.

## 9. Output format (what the LLM must return for each company)

```
SUBJECT:
TO: / BCC: (as given in the research block)
BODY:
FOLLOW-UP 1 (day 3, same thread, 30-60 words): a second idea or a peer example (e.g. the CapCut contest-to-prize path)
FOLLOW-UP 2 (day 8 sponsor / day 6 organizer): a real date (sponsor: 2027 entries close 15 Feb; organizer: WAiFF validates a licensee's dates 60 days ahead, so a December edition needs a decision by mid-October)
FOLLOW-UP 3 (day 17 sponsor / day 10 organizer): breakup plus referral: "Should I close the loop on this? If someone else owns festival partnerships at {Company}, a name would help, and I won't follow up again."
LINKEDIN NOTE (under 200 characters): "Hi {first}, I'm India Ambassador for the World AI Film Festival (Palais des Festivals, Cannes). Saw {short hook}. Would love to connect. Aditya"
LINKEDIN AFTER ACCEPT: msg 1 (a question, no pitch), msg 2 (the idea), msg 3 (ask for the right person). Each under 400 characters.
WORD COUNT OF BODY:
CHECKLIST: PASS/FAIL for each item in section 10
```

Follow-ups go in the same thread and never start with "Following up on the note below". Never follow up after a decline or a bounce.

**Reply rule.** When a person replies, answer within 24 hours.
- Answer exactly what they asked, in 120 words or less.
- Send a calendar invite yourself for one specific slot in their time zone.
- Never a one-liner, never an essay, never argue with a decline.

## 10. Checklist (every email must pass before sending)

1. TO is a named decision-maker. BCC is at most 2 named people, on the same domain. There are no role inboxes.
2. Every address is verified and has never bounced. The company is not on the do-not-repeat list.
3. The subject is 2 to 5 words, lowercase, with no banned word and no em dash, and it matches line 1.
4. Line 1 is a dated fact about them. It is not flattery.
5. There is one idea, one proof point and one question.
6. The sponsor body is 50 to 90 words (the organizer body is up to 110).
7. Every WAiFF fact is from section 2. Nothing is from section 3.
8. There is no price, deck, attachment, link other than the website, ROI or reach number.
9. There are no em dashes, semicolons, exclamation marks or hype words.
10. The greeting uses the TO person's first name.
11. The close and signature are exactly as in section 7.
12. It is scheduled for 8 to 11am in their time zone on a weekday, and logged the same day.
