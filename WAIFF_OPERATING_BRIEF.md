**Address rule — keep this fast.** If the address appears on the company's own website — a partnership page, sponsorship page, press or media contact page, contact-us page, or an official press release — take it and move on. **Rules added 3 Sep 2026, after analysing every send since 2 Sep against its bounces and replies (full findings in section 6a):**

- **Never guess a personal address by pattern** (`firstname.lastname@company.com`, `firstinitiallastname@`, etc.) unless you found that exact address written down somewhere — a bio page, a press release byline, a company directory. Guessed personal addresses were the single biggest source of bounces (Lenovo: 3 for 3 bounced; Adani: 4 of 12 guessed BCC addresses bounced). If you can't find the address written down, use the company's published inbox instead — it lands, even if it's less personal.
- **Never use a personal email provider (gmail.com, yahoo.com, etc.) as a company contact.** If that's the only address you can find for someone, it isn't a company channel — treat it as "no channel found" and move to LinkedIn instead.
- **One company, one domain, in one BCC list — no exceptions, including that company's own outside PR or ad agency.** The Sennheiser send BCC'd 21 addresses across sennheiser.com, sonova.com, neumann.com, and three unrelated agency domains (pistonagency.com, groundagency.com, gasolinemedia.com) in a single message. That is exactly the "blast" pattern section 8 already warns about, just spread across a client and its agencies instead of across unrelated companies. Treat an agency domain as a different company — leave it out.
- **A different TLD is a different domain, not the same company.** The Perplexity send BCC'd business@/india@/support@/api@/publishers@perplexity.**ai** together with assistant@perplexity.**com** — one letter of TLD difference, but a genuinely separate mail system. The .com address bounced outright while the .ai addresses landed and triggered a stack of bot/support auto-replies (a publisher-program bot, an Intercom bot, and a support reply saying the sender's email didn't match a Perplexity account) — message technically delivered, functionally lost to routing. Same lesson as Sennheiser, confirmed again: verify the TLD matches before adding any address to a BCC list, don't assume company name = domain.
- **Every send needs a real address in To:, never `undisclosed-recipients:` with everything in BCC.** Tech Mahindra, Freshworks, Zoho, Sennheiser, BenQ, Adani AI Labs and Adani Digital Labs all went out this way. It reads as a blast to the recipient and to spam filters, and there's no accountable single desk that owns the message. If you only have a shared inbox, put that one address in To: and use "Dear Respected Team" — don't pad it with BCC guesses to compensate.
- **Check what a plausible-sounding address is actually for before using it.** Zoho's `partner-operations@` sounds like sponsorship but is the channel/reseller partner program — it auto-replied pointing to a reseller sign-up page, not a human who handles event sponsorship. A "Partner" or "Alliances" address at a large enterprise software company is very often the technology/reseller channel, not brand partnerships — read the auto-reply or the page it lives on before trusting the name.
- **A press/PR-tier address is a legitimate last-resort To:, not just a BCC add-on, at a company with no separate sponsorship inbox** — NVIDIA's `india-pr@nvidia.com`, Nikon's `NindSupport@nikon.com`, and Vidu's `support@vidu.com` all reached a real person who read the message and either engaged or forwarded it internally. The difference from the old "BCC press only" rule: this applies only when you've confirmed there's no dedicated partnerships/sponsorship address at all, and the subject line has to say "Partnership Opportunity" plainly so it isn't filed as a press query.

## 10c. THE 48-HOUR REVIEW

Every two days, review and report on:

1. **Deliverability** — bounces, and whether replies are still arriving. A day of total silence after a run of replies is the early warning that mail is landing in spam.
2. **Reply rate by segment** — which of the target buckets in section 5 actually answer. Reallocate tomorrow's 100 toward what is replying.

4. **Opening lines** — which appreciation angles draw a response. Specific product detail usually beats general praise; confirm it against the data rather than assuming.

**Sample-size rule.** A change of strategy needs at least **100 sends** behind it, not 48 hours of data. ## 15. WARM FOLLOW-UP PLAYBOOK — added 4 Sep 2026, from the Simon Horsman (Quilty) email

This is a different animal from the cold sponsor/organizer outreach the rest of this brief covers. It applies whenever Aditya has already had a real meeting or call with someone and is writing the follow-up — a warm, one-to-one message to a named person he's actually spoken with, not a first-touch cold send. Worked through end-to-end on Simon Horsman (CEO & Co-Founder, Quilty — an entertainment-financing "intelligence layer" platform, met via a real call) as the first case. Rules learned from that pass:

- **Personalize from real research, not from the pitch.** Open with something specific and true about the person — a real credit, a real project, their own words back to them — found by actually looking them up (their LinkedIn About/Experience/Projects, company site, tagline), not generic flattery. The opener is what earns the next line getting read.
- **No em-dashes, no corporate cadence.** Contractions, short sentences, natural rhythm — write it the way a founder would actually type it, not the way a press release would. This is the single biggest lever for making a message read as human instead of AI-generated.
- **Data: a few killer numbers, woven into sentences, not a stat dump.** Two or three figures max (the standout growth number, the founder credential, the scale number) — more than that and the reader's eyes glaze over and they stop reading. Comprehensive does not mean exhaustive; it means the right facts, not all the facts.
- **Frame the fit reciprocally, and only claim what was actually confirmed in the meeting.** State plainly what's in it for them, not just the ask — but any specific claim about how their own product/company works should come from what they said on the call, not from inference off a tagline or LinkedIn blurb. Getting their own product wrong in the follow-up reads worse than saying nothing specific at all — always flag an inferred claim back to Aditya for a gut-check before it goes out.
- **No hard sell, no pressure CTA.** Close with something like "nothing needs deciding off this email" rather than a call to action. The read Aditya wants is confident-founder-with-data, not salesperson-with-an-ask.
- **Attachments/portfolio links go at the very end, below the website link — never at the top.** Leading with a Drive link invites the reader to skip straight to "click, skim, judge" and never read the actual message. The link is the reward for reading, not the opener.
- **Verify link permissions before sending.** Checked the Drive folder's sharing settings (`get_file_permissions`) before handing over the link — confirmed "anyone with the link" read access — rather than assuming.
- Aditya's own read on whether a message like this "worked": he's not trying to control whether the recipient likes it, and he's right not to — a warm follow-up to someone who's already going to do his own diligence just needs to be honest, specific, and not sound like a pitch. That's the bar, not a guessed reaction.

## 16. COLD OUTREACH COPYWRITING STANDARDS — added 4 Sep 2026, from a direct audit against sourced 2026 benchmarks

Every cold sponsor/organizer email sent through 3-4 Sep used one template structure repeated verbatim in shape across ~70 companies: same subject-line pattern ("Partnership Opportunity with WAIFF India — [Company] x World AI Film Festival"), same paragraph order (personalized opener → full WAiFF backstory paragraph → soft ask), same length (180-260 words). Benchmarked against real 2026 cold-email data, this has three concrete, fixable problems:

**1. Too long.** Elite-tier cold emails (top 10% by reply rate, 10.7%+) run under 80 words; the broader recommended range is 75-150. Ours run 2-3x that. [Source: Instantly.ai 2026 Cold Email Benchmark Report]

**2. The ask is buried and vague.** "State your purpose in the first sentence, not the third" — ours state it in the third paragraph, after a full company-history recap the recipient didn't ask for. The closing line ("Would love to explore a partnership... happy to share sponsorship tiers if useful") is also not a real CTA — a real CTA is single, binary, and low-friction: the benchmark's top performer was literally "Would you have a couple minutes to chat about this over the next few days?" [Source: Instantly.ai; Sendspark]

**3. Identical structure across ~70 sends is a spam-filter and credibility risk, not just a style nitpick.** "Inbox providers now use AI detection to filter pattern-matched templates" — sending the same shape of email to 70 different companies is exactly the pattern that gets caught. Subject lines fare the same way: research shows subject lines of four words or less, in natural lowercase-feeling language, get meaningfully higher opens than a long formulaic pattern repeated every time; personalized subject lines see up to 50% higher opens than generic ones. [Source: Mixmax 2026; Sendspark]

**Revised template, effective for all sends from 4 Sep 2026 onward:**

- **Subject:** short, specific, varies per send — not a fixed template phrase repeated verbatim. Reference the real hook, not "Partnership Opportunity." (e.g. "the AI cinema thing I mentioned" is too casual for cold; better: "WAiFF x [Company] — Cannes to Mumbai" or a short question tied to their own recent news.)
- **Line 1:** the personalized, researched hook — no change, this part already works and stays.
- **Line 2, in the SAME breath as the hook:** who WAiFF is and the ask, compressed into 1-2 sentences, not a full paragraph. Cut the founder bio / leadership names / press coverage from the first email entirely — save it for when they ask or reply. Two numbers maximum (e.g. "6,000+ submissions, 100+ countries" is plenty; drop the rest).
- **Line 3: one binary CTA.** Not "happy to share sponsorship tiers if useful" — ask a real yes/no question: "Worth a 15-minute call this week?" or "Should I send over the sponsorship deck?" **[Superseded 24 Sep 2026: never offer the deck in a cold email. Use an interest question. See section 21.]**
- **Total length target: 80-120 words, sign-off included.** If it doesn't fit, cut the WAiFF description further, not the CTA.
- **Vary sentence structure and paragraph order company to company** — same information, different shape, so it doesn't read as a mail-merge even when it technically is one.
- This does not apply retroactively to the ~70 already sent (no value in re-litigating those), but governs every new send from here on, and is worth testing: if reply rates on the new shorter format noticeably beat the old format over the next 1-2 weeks, that is itself useful signal to record in the session log.

**On reaching the right team (the other half of "communicate to the right people"):** section 6/6a/8/8a already cover channel-verification discipline (no guessed emails, same-domain BCC only, checking what an address is actually for) in depth — that discipline is the main lever for landing in the right inbox, more than copywriting is. Nothing new to add there beyond what's already standing; the fix this section adds is about what happens once the email lands, not where it lands.

## 21. MAIL QUALITY STANDARD, 24 Sep 2026 (supersedes sections 7, 10 and 16 where they conflict)

Built from a forensic audit of all ~990 campaign emails in Gmail, a sourced best-practice study, and a verified WAiFF fact book. Full playbook with templates, subject lines and examples: **`WAIFF_Email_Quality_Playbook_Sep24.md`**. The pre-send checklist in that file is mandatory.

1. **Targeting before copy.** Write to a named owner of budget, activation or new IP. Never press@, media@, support@, care@, info@, helpdesk@, reservations@, investor relations or the company secretary. (Nearly half of recipients so far were role inboxes.)
2. **One company, one sequence.** Never pitch a company again under a new subject. Up to 3 people per company, each in a separate, role-specific email, 2 to 3 days apart. **No CC, no BCC.**
3. **Subject:** 2 to 5 words, lowercase or sentence case, names them or one concrete asset. Banned: "opportunity", "partnership", "sponsorship", "X x WAiFF", em dashes, prices, fake "Re:".
4. **Body:** 50 to 90 words (organizer up to 110). Line 1 is a dated fact about them. One WAiFF fact, one specific idea built for them, one proof point, one interest question ("Worth a look?", "Open to a one-page outline?"). No meeting demand, no calendar link, no deck, no ROI or reach numbers.
5. **Signature:** Aditya Upadhyay / India Ambassador, World AI Film Festival / worldaifilmfestival.com / +91 mobile (WhatsApp). One link only.
6. **Follow-ups:** in the same thread, each adds something new (a second idea, a peer example, a real date). Never "Following up on the note below". Sponsor: day 3, day 8, day 17 (breakup with referral ask). Organizer: day 3, day 6, day 10.
7. **Sending:** 20 to 40 new emails a day, weekdays only, 8:00 to 11:00 in the recipient's time zone (use Gmail schedule-send), never in bursts. Never mail an address that has bounced. Verify addresses before sending.
8. **Top priority fix:** ask WAiFF HQ (Marco / Studio Laffitte) for an official mailbox such as aditya@ or india@worldaifilmfestival.com. Custom-domain senders get about twice the replies of free Gmail, and it proves you represent WAiFF.
