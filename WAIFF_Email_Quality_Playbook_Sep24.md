## Part A. What went wrong, in numbers

The copy was not the only problem, or even the main one. Targeting, sending behaviour and factual accuracy did more damage.

| # | Finding | Evidence from Gmail | Why it kills replies |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Wrong inboxes** | Only 26 of 570 first emails (4.6%) went to a partnerships, sponsorship or BD address. About half of all recipients were press@, media@, support@, care@, info@, helpdesk@, investor relations or company secretaries. Replies came back as "we only respond to editorial requests" (Qatar Airways), "Dear Customer" tickets, AI support bots (InVideo, AssemblyAI), and a DNEG auto-reply about job applications. | These desks cannot buy a sponsorship. The Kimi and Hedra declines each got a 1,000 to 1,500-word rebuttal. | Senior buyers spot it instantly and stop reading. |
| 15 | **Do-not-contact breaches** | 9 excluded companies were pitched: MiniMax, TF1, Fnac, Station F and CapCut ("extending your existing partnership"), plus JioStar, ZEE, Eros and CloudWalk. | It embarrasses WAiFF with its own partners. |

**The score after 20 days:**
- 13 companies engaged (2.7%) and 30 companies sent a human reply (6.3%).
- At least 6 calls were held: Radisson, YuVerse, IA-Meetings, Sennheiser's agency, MCI and Filmustage.
- Zero reached a proposal.
- The best-performing subject style was the plain "[Company] x World AI Film Festival, India 2026": 5 human replies from 67 sends.

**Who engaged:** 10 of the 13 interested companies make products for filmmakers or creators, or run events. The other 3 were India marketing heads answering an India pitch. The 88 luxury, finance and global-brand emails of 17 to 23 Sep produced zero engaged replies. That is why the v5 list is tech and creator companies with a proven festival or event habit.

**What actually worked** (keep doing this):
- A single, correct, named address (EPAM, Radisson).
- One real, specific fact about the company (Nikon ZR via RED, the Havells "Hawa Badlegi" campaign).
- Short follow-ups that added one new fact and one clear ask (the 14 Sep Qube follow-up and the 22 Sep "two more editions" note).
- Asking for the right person. It appeared once, too late, and still drew the ILM reply.

## Part B. The standard: 12 rules

1. **Write to a named person who owns budget, activation or new IP.** That means CMO, brand or partnerships head, creator marketing head or country MD for sponsors, and CEO, head of IP or head of live events for organizers. Never press@, media@, support@, care@, info@, helpdesk@, reservations@, investor relations or the company secretary.
2. **One company, one sequence.** Never pitch a company again under a new subject. If you want a second person, write them a separate email 2 to 3 days later, with an angle that fits their role. Maximum 3 people per company.
3. **No CC. No BCC.** Ever.
4. **Subject: 2 to 5 words, lowercase or sentence case,** naming them or one concrete asset. No "opportunity", "partnership", "sponsorship", "X x WAiFF", em dashes, prices or fake "Re:". (Part D)
5. **50 to 90 words** for sponsors, up to 110 for organizers. Grade 5 reading level. Short sentences.
6. **Five lines:** a dated fact about them, one WAiFF fact, one idea built for them, one proof point, one interest question. (Part E)
7. **Ask for interest, not a meeting.** Use "Worth a look?" or "Open to a one-page outline?". Interest questions book about twice the meetings of meeting requests (Gong). Offer times only after they reply.
8. **Only verified facts** (Part C). No price, tier, deck, reach number or ROI claim. Promise measurement, not outcomes.
9. **Follow up in the same thread, with something new each time.** Never "following up on the note below". (Part F)
10. **Send 20 to 40 new emails a day, weekdays only, 8 to 11am in their time zone,** with Gmail schedule-send and a few minutes between emails. Never mail a bounced address again. Verify addresses before sending.
11. **Signature:** Aditya Upadhyay / India Ambassador, World AI Film Festival / worldaifilmfestival.com / +91 mobile (WhatsApp). Type the link as plain text, never pasted from a rendered email. Add one opt-out line: "Not relevant? Just say so and I won't follow up."
12. **No WAiFF work email is available, so make Gmail look trustworthy.** Use one consistent signature, plain text and one clean link. Keep your LinkedIn headline matching ("India Ambassador, World AI Film Festival") so people can verify you. Keep volume low and addresses verified.

3. **One idea built for them:** a named award, a branded challenge, a jury seat, a demo, an official partner role, or the India licence. Take it from the "Activation idea" column.

## Part F. Follow-ups

**Rules:**
- Reply in the same thread.
- Each follow-up adds one new thing: a second idea, a peer example, a real date or a measurement plan.
- 30 to 60 words.
- Never "following up on the note below", "just checking in", "circling back" or "did you get a chance".
- Never follow up after a decline. Never follow up a dead address.

**Where replies come from:**
- 58% come from the first email and 42% from follow-ups (Instantly 2026).
- The first follow-up has the highest single-step reply rate (Belkins).
- Four or more follow-ups triple spam complaints.

## Part G. When they reply

**Replies come before new outreach.** Every human reply gets an answer within 24 hours (aim for 2 working hours), before any new cold email goes out that day.

**The reply standard:**
- Answer the exact question they asked, in 120 words or less, written by hand.
- Send the calendar invite yourself, from the same address, for one specific slot in their time zone. Put a second slot in the email.
- Never a one-liner ("This is my number", "tuesday, thursday, friday works"), and never a 1,000-word essay.
- Never argue with a decline. Reply once: "Understood, thank you. May I check back when {their reason} changes?"
- Keep the tone professional. Never "just relax", "I get hundreds of calls" or "your own organisation is based out of AI".

**Recommended rule change (needs your OK, because it changes brief section 3).** Today the brief says no price in any message. But the two prospects who asked for material (Cascadeur and NVIDIA) asked for a price range, what's included, the date and proof, and the rule withheld exactly that. Recommendation: when a prospect *explicitly asks*, send a one-page summary within 24 hours. It covers what they get, the dates, and the official package range from the 2027 deck (after Marco confirms which card applies), plus a call slot. Keep cold emails price-free.

- **Positive:** propose two times in *their* time zone ("Tue 10:00 or Wed 16:00 CET?"), plus "or I can call or WhatsApp". Do not send the deck.
- **"Send me the deck / proposal":** "Happy to. So I send the right thing: for 2027, is it creator adoption, brand presence in Cannes, or India that matters most? Two words is enough." Then send a one-page note in plain words (their objective, the one asset, how it would be measured) and walk through the deck live on a call.
- **"Not now / budget is set":** "Understood. When does 2027 planning open, and who owns it?" Log a reminder.
- **Routed by a support desk:** "Thank you. Who handles brand partnerships for festivals and events?" Then write fresh to that person, saying the team pointed you to them.
- **Venue or vendor sales replies** (hotels, exhibition centres, Getty's paid services): log them as vendors, not prospects.
- **Decline:** thank them, close the thread, mark the company closed. Never re-pitch it under a new subject.
- **Calls:** get five answers. What they need to sell. The metric they're judged on. What they've tried. The action they want customers to take. Their budget range. The follow-up gives one idea against those answers, never a menu of tiers.

## Part J. Pre-send checklist (every email must pass)

**Targeting**
1. A named decision-maker, not a role inbox.
2. The address is verified and has never bounced.
3. The company is not on the contacted or do-not-contact lists, and has never had a WAiFF email under another subject.
4. No CC and no BCC.

**Subject**

5. 2 to 5 words, no banned words, no em dash, no fake "Re:". It matches line 1.

**Body**

6. Line 1 is a dated, specific fact about them.
7. One idea, one proof point, one interest question.
8. 50 to 90 words (organizers up to 110). No sentence over about 20 words.
9. Every WAiFF fact is in Part C1. Nothing from C2.
10. No price, tier, deck, attachment, reach number or ROI claim. (The organizer licence fee only once the currency is confirmed, and ideally on the call.)
11. No em dashes, semicolons, exclamation marks, ALL CAPS or emojis.
12. No AI-sounding phrases: "I hope this email finds you well", "Greetings of the day", "truly remarkable", "impressed", "innovative", "synergy", "leverage", "delighted", "Dear Respected Team", "Hi team" to a named person.
13. The link is typed as plain text (worldaifilmfestival.com), not a Google redirect.
14. Opt-out line and the standard signature with a WhatsApp number.

**Sending**

15. Scheduled for 8 to 11am in their time zone, on a weekday.
16. Logged in the tracker the same day. Company, person, subject and send date recorded.
