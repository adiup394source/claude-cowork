# WAiFF India — Global (Non-India) B2B Sponsorship Marketplace/Brokerage Platforms

Research date: September 22, 2026
Scope: Genuine event–sponsor marketplaces or commission-based brokerages, based **anywhere except India**, that take a percentage cut only (or mainly) when a sponsorship deal actually closes. Complements the earlier India-focused search and the already-verified **SponsorMyEvent** (sponsormyevent.com — not re-researched here).

Method: Web search across "sponsorship marketplace," "event sponsor matching," "sponsorship broker commission," G2/Capterra/CB Insights competitor listings, plus direct verification of each candidate's own site (What it is / fee structure / signup requirements / blockers). Many "sponsorship software" hits turned out to be proposal-builders (Sponseasy-type), CRM/management tools for deals already won (HelloSponsor, SponsorCX), or plain directories (UK Sponsorship Database) — these were excluded per your brief. What follows are the ones that survived that filter.

---

## 1. Anvara
**URL:** https://www.anvara.com/ (rightsholder/organizer track: https://www.anvara.com/rightsholders/ — signup at https://app.anvara.com/)
**Country:** United States (founded May 2024; planning European expansion within 12–18 months)

**What it actually is:** A genuine two-sided marketplace ("the operating system for sponsorship deals — start to finish") covering sports, festivals, and live events, with an AI matching tool for brands and roughly $1B in listed sponsorship inventory. Organizers ("rightsholders") list opportunities and receive inbound offers from brands; deals are negotiated, signed, and paid for inside the platform, with Anvara holding funds in escrow until deal milestones are met. This is a real matchmaking marketplace, not a directory.

**Fee structure:** Listing and managing inventory is **free**. Anvara states outright: *"We only charge a success-based fee"* — i.e., it only gets paid when a sponsorship deal closes, mirroring SponsorMyEvent's model. The exact percentage is not published; you have to contact them directly ("generally well below typical sales commission rates" per a co-founder interview).

**Signup requirements:** No stated company-email-domain requirement in the public content reviewed. However, signup is **not self-serve** — app.anvara.com is a "Request Access" gate, and Anvara explicitly says *"Approval is typically instant after a short call."*

**Blockers:** The requirement for a screening call before an account is approved is the main blocker — a human (Aditya) will need to do that call regardless of who fills the initial form. No CAPTCHA was visible in the fetched content, but this couldn't be confirmed by automated browsing.

## 2. Sponsoo
**URL:** https://www.sponsoo.com/
**Country:** Germany (Hamburg), serving multiple regions/languages (EN, DE, ES, FR, IT, etc.), with local pages for LA, NYC, SF, London, Rome.

**What it actually is:** Billed as "The #1 marketplace for sports sponsorship," but the sign-up flow explicitly includes an **Events** category alongside athletes/teams/clubs/associations, so event organizers can create a sponsorship profile and be discovered by sponsors browsing the marketplace. This is a real, established, functioning marketplace (in operation for years, took a Series A).

**Fee structure:** Confirmed **20% commission**, charged only when a sponsorship deal is sold/closes through the platform (Sponsoo's own glossary example: a €1,000 deal nets them €200). No exclusivity required to list.

**Signup requirements:** No company-email-domain requirement found — signup supports **social login (Facebook, Google, XING)** in addition to email, which strongly suggests a personal Gmail account works fine.

**Blockers:** None identified beyond a standard signup flow. Could not confirm CAPTCHA presence without live browser testing — a human will need to complete final account creation either way.

## 3. Sponsor Seeker
**URL:** https://sponsorseeker.co.uk/ (listing form: https://sponsorseeker.co.uk/find-sponsorship/sponsorship-listing-form/)
**Country:** United Kingdom ("the UK's biggest online marketplace for sponsorship opportunities")

**What it actually is:** A real marketplace/brokerage hybrid — organizations register, build a profile (presentation, video, social links), and get matched with sponsors actively looking for opportunities; there's also a support phone line, suggesting some active matchmaking effort behind the listings, not just a passive database.

**Fee structure:** Three tiers —
- Database Listing: **free**
- Standard Listing: **£25 + VAT**
- Featured Listing: **£125 + VAT**
- Plus, critically: **"all of our listings attract a 10% success fee on any sponsorship gained through the site."** — this is the closest direct match to SponsorMyEvent's model found in this research (10% on close, same figure Aditya already knows).

**Signup requirements:** Listing form asks for organization details, opportunity description, images, and social links — no explicit company-email-domain requirement found in the content reviewed.

**Blockers:** Could not verify CAPTCHA presence via text fetch. A small paid-listing tier exists if the free database listing gets deprioritized in matching — worth asking them directly before paying anything.

## 4. CommunitySponsor
**URL:** https://www.communitysponsor.org/
**Country:** United States (Miami, FL — HQ and current event coverage)

**What it actually is:** A genuine transactional marketplace: organizers "create your event and sponsorship packages in minutes," and CommunitySponsor sends the sponsorship offer to local businesses actively looking to sponsor community events, with secure in-platform payment processing.

**Fee structure:** Pure pay-per-transaction, **no upfront cost** — "you only pay when you earn." Three selectable tiers per event:
- Standard: 10% commission
- Featured: 25% commission
- Concierge: 50% commission
(plus a flat 5% fee on any ticket sales, and Stripe's standard ~3% card processing on top).

**Signup requirements:** Not specified; no evidence of a company-email requirement.

**Blockers/caveats:** This is the most clearly commission-only structure found, but it currently reads as **Miami/local-community-focused** (their listed events are Aventura, Miami Lakes, etc.) — it's unclear whether they'd take on an international film festival, and their sponsor base is described as "local businesses." Worth a direct email to hello@communitysponsor.org to check if they'll take a Mumbai-based festival before investing time, or treat this as lower priority / a secondary option.

## 5. SponsorPitch
**URL:** https://sponsorpitch.com/ (app/pricing: https://app.sponsorpitch.com/pricing)
**Country:** United States

**What it actually is:** A real, long-running sponsorship marketplace/database — organizers get a searchable database of 13,000+ brands with real-time deal data and decision-maker contacts, plus a "Pitch Board" where opportunities can be listed for visibility to brands. This is genuine matchmaking infrastructure, not a lead-gen content site.

**Fee structure — IMPORTANT MISMATCH:** SponsorPitch is **subscription-based, not commission-based.** Tiers run Free → Prospector ($39–54/mo) → Professional ($83–134/mo) → Agency ($221–334/mo, per team). There is **no percentage-of-deal fee** — you pay monthly regardless of whether a sponsorship closes. Flagging this clearly since it does not match the "10%-on-close" model Aditya is looking for, but it's included because it's a legitimate, well-established marketplace that might still be useful (their free tier could be worth a look) and because it shows up repeatedly as a real competitor/benchmark to SponsorMyEvent.

**Signup requirements:** Not explicitly stated; generic "Sign Up Free" CTA.

**Blockers:** None identified beyond standard signup. Its full value likely requires a paid tier.

## 6. WW Sponsorship (wwsponsorship.com)
**URL:** https://wwsponsorship.com/sponsorship-sales-agency/
**Country:** Canada (Kanata, Ontario)

**What it actually is:** A genuine commission-eligible **brokerage/agency**, not a self-serve marketplace — they act as an "external revenue partner" doing prospect identification, outreach, proposal creation, negotiation, and deal closing on the client's behalf. This fits the "agency that actively sources sponsors for events on a commission basis" half of your brief.

**Fee structure:** Not standardized/published — they state fees "vary based on scope, deal complexity, and revenue model used," citing monthly retainers, performance-based commission, or hybrid models as common approaches. No flat "10%" figure confirmed; requires a custom consultation to get real numbers.

**Signup requirements:** No self-serve signup — engagement starts with a free consultation call (info@wwsponsorship.com / +1 613-716-9373).

**Blockers:** Not an instant online signup at all — it's a sales conversation, so a human (Aditya) must handle outreach and negotiation from the start. Include this only if you're open to a bespoke agency relationship rather than a self-serve marketplace listing.

## Shortlist: prioritize these first

1. **Sponsor Seeker (UK)** — closest match to SponsorMyEvent's exact model: free base listing + explicit **10% success fee only on sponsorship gained through the site**, plus an active-sounding matching process (not just a static directory). Start here.
2. **Sponsoo (Germany/Europe)** — established, real marketplace with an Events category, confirmed **20% commission-on-close**, free signup with social login (no company-email barrier). Strong second choice, especially if you want EU-market sponsor reach.
3. **Anvara (US)** — the most modern/well-funded of the group, explicitly success-fee-only with escrow-protected payments, but signup requires a short approval call rather than instant self-serve — worth pursuing for its scale (~$1B inventory) once someone is ready to take that call.
4. **CommunitySponsor (US)** — cleanest "pay only when you earn" commission structure (10/25/50% tiers, no upfront cost) of everything reviewed, but confirm by email whether they'll onboard a non-US, non-local (Mumbai) film festival before investing signup time.

*(SponsorPitch and WW Sponsorship are documented above for completeness but are lower priority: SponsorPitch is subscription-only, not commission-based, and WW Sponsorship is a bespoke agency requiring a sales call rather than a marketplace listing.)*

## Addendum, Sep 22 (2nd pass): re-prioritized for €50,000–€1,000,000+ deal sizes

You specifically want platforms that can land sponsorships from €50k up to seven figures, not small local-business deals. Re-ranking the 6 above against that bar, plus 3 new candidates checked and ruled out:

**Best fit for this deal size:**

1. **Anvara (US)** — still the top pick. ~$1B in listed sponsorship inventory across sports, festivals and live events, AI matching, deals negotiated and paid for in-platform with funds held in escrow until milestones are met. This is built for real commercial-scale deals, not small community sponsorships. Success-fee only (percentage undisclosed, "well below typical sales commission"). Requires a short approval call before you get an account — worth doing.
2. **WW Sponsorship (Canada)** — a genuine agency, not self-serve. They do prospect identification, outreach, proposal-building and closing on your behalf, with fees that flex by deal size and complexity (retainer, commission, or hybrid). Because it's a real agency relationship rather than a fixed-fee marketplace, it's the one most naturally suited to a €400k-scale Global Partner conversation, not just a €50k license. Starts with a free consultation call.
3. **SponsorPitch (US)** — not commission-based (it's a $39–334/month subscription), so it doesn't match your "they take a cut" requirement, but flagging it anyway because its core asset is a searchable database of 13,000+ brands with real decision-maker contacts and real-time deal data — genuinely useful for finding who at a large company actually signs off on six/seven-figure sponsorships, even if you'd still close the deal yourself.

**Downgraded for this size range:**

- **CommunitySponsor (US)** — cleanest commission structure of the original six (10/25/50% tiers), but their own site describes their sponsor base as "local businesses" around Miami. Not a fit for €50k+ deals. Deprioritize for WAiFF.
- **Sponsor Seeker (UK)** and **Sponsoo (Germany)** — both real, both commission-based (10% and 20% respectively), but neither states a typical deal-size range, and both read as general-purpose marketplaces rather than large-ticket specialists. Worth keeping in the mix but don't expect them to be where a €1M conversation starts.

**Checked and ruled out this pass:**

- **Eventsorship** — looked promising in search results (claims "mid-six-figure average deals," a 24-month $100M GMV target) but on inspection its own site is a business-plan document, not an operating platform: no founder names, no real contact email (it's a placeholder, "info@example.com"), no press, no evidence of a single closed deal. Treat as not real / not yet launched. Excluded.
- **SponsorFlo** — turned out to be subscription software ($299–$2,700/month) for properties managing sponsorships they already sell themselves, not a marketplace that finds you new sponsors, and explicitly "not a marketplace." Wrong category. Excluded.
- **Instigo Agency** — its own site blocks automated access, so its fee structure and legitimacy couldn't be verified; a same-named product on G2 ("Instigo Audiences") turned out to be an unrelated Facebook/Instagram ad-targeting tool, which raises doubt about which "Instigo" is real. Excluded pending Aditya checking it directly rather than recommending it unverified.

**Bottom line:** for the €50k–€1M range specifically, Anvara and WW Sponsorship are the two worth your time first. Sponsor Seeker and Sponsoo are reasonable backups. CommunitySponsor should come off the list for WAiFF.
