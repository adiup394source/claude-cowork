## 3. What went wrong in the first 20 days (never repeat this)

~990 emails to ~478 companies produced 30 human replies (6.3%), 13 engaged (2.7%), 0 proposals and 0 money. The causes:

1. **Wrong inboxes.** Only 4.6% went to a partnerships or sponsorship address. The rest hit press@, support@, care@, info@, helpdesk@, investor relations and company secretaries. Most human replies were hotels selling rooms to us.
2. **Wrong targets:**
   - Seed-stage startups with no budget.
   - Chip and B2B enterprise firms.
   - Government bodies.
   - Hotels (vendors, not sponsors).
   - VFX houses that dislike AI.
   - Industrial groups offered the organizer licence through their company secretary.
   - Small event agencies that could not fund a December edition.
3. **Spam behaviour:**
   - 40% of emails were sent between midnight and 4am IST, and 50% on weekends.
   - 135 emails went in 4 minutes, and 235 in one day.
   - 274 follow-ups had a **fake "Re:"** subject, and 135 said "following up on the note below" with no note below.
   - BCC sprays of up to 20 addresses went out, and 24 emails had everyone in BCC.
   - 8.9% of first emails bounced, and dead addresses were re-mailed up to 4 times.
   - 77 companies were pitched again under new subjects, including 9 do-not-contact partners (MiniMax, TF1, Fnac, Station F, CapCut, JioStar, ZEE, Eros, CloudWalk).
4. **Bad copy:**
   - 136 to 1,008 words, at college reading level, with the ask always in the last line.
   - 72% of subjects had em dashes.
   - Phrases like "Partnership Opportunity", "I hope this email finds you well" and "truly remarkable".
   - Google redirect links instead of the real URL.
   - A CEO said the reply read as AI-written.
5. **Wrong facts**:
   - "Jury President Gong Li" (she was festival President).
   - "100+ countries", "15+ countries, 30+ cities", "third Cannes edition" (2025 was in Nice) and "Apple Global".
   - Ginésy named as co-founder.
   - Hollywood names with no source (McConaughey, Howard, Cameron).
   - A merge bug telling about 54 companies "[Company]'s India edition is launching in Mumbai".
   - Prices and "media value vs budget" lines in sponsor emails, and decks sent as links.
6. **Warm leads lost.**
   - Cascadeur asked for a price range and waited 10 days.
   - Cooke Optics got 1,100 words after 82 hours.
   - Wonder Studios got "tuesday, thursday, friday" with no date.
   - ShareChat got a phone number and then a wall of text.
   - Only 2 of 13 got a calendar invite, and nobody got a one-page proposal.

## 5. How every email is written now (full detail in the playbook)

1. **To:** a named person who owns budget, activation or new IP:
   - Sponsors: CMO, brand or partnerships head, creator marketing head, country MD.
   - Organizers: CEO, head of IP or live events, strategy head.

   Never press@, support@, care@, info@, helpdesk@, IR or the company secretary. Apollo supplies the addresses, and each must be verified.
2. **One company, one sequence.** Never pitch again under a new subject. Up to 3 people per company, in separate emails, 2 to 3 days apart. **No CC, no BCC, ever.**
3. **Subject:** 2 to 5 words, lowercase, names them or one asset ("picsart award in cannes", "nodwin and waiff india"). Banned: opportunity, partnership, sponsorship, "X x WAiFF", em dash, price, fake "Re:".
4. **Body:** 50 to 90 words (organizers up to 110), in five lines:
   1. A dated fact about them.
   2. One WAiFF fact.
   3. One idea built for them.
   4. One proof point.
   5. An interest question ("Worth a look?", "Open to a one-page outline?", "Would a 20-minute look be useful?").
5. **Facts: only from playbook Part C1.**
   - "More than 5,000 AI films from over 80 countries" (2026).
   - "Third edition, 6 and 7 April 2027, Palais des Festivals, Cannes".
   - "Gong Li presided over the 2026 edition".
   - "Founded by Marco Landi, former President and COO of Apple".
   - "Supported by the City of Cannes and the Alpes-Maritimes Department".
   - "CapCut and MiniMax presented named prizes on stage".
   - "Three Indian creators were Cannes 2026 finalists".
   - "Kyoto, Seoul and Istanbul are run by local licensees".
   - Everything in Part C2 is banned.
6. **Never:** a price in cold email, a deck or Canva link, an attachment, ROI or reach numbers, em dashes, semicolons, "I hope this email finds you well", "Hi team" to a named person, or text that sounds AI-written. Write it like a founder typing.
7. **Signature:** Aditya Upadhyay / India Ambassador, World AI Film Festival / worldaifilmfestival.com (typed as plain text) / +91 mobile (WhatsApp). Add one opt-out line: "Not relevant? Just say so and I won't follow up."
8. **Follow-ups**, in the same thread, each adding something new:
   - **Sponsor:** day 3 (second idea, such as a CapCut-style contest), day 8 (real date: 2027 entries close 15 February), day 17 (breakup and ask for the right person).
   - **Organizer:** day 3 (revenue formula), day 6 (60-day validation deadline, so a decision by mid-October), day 10 (breakup).
   - Never "following up on the note below". Never after a decline or a bounce.
9. **Replies come first.** Answer every human reply within 24 hours, before any new outreach:
   - 120 words or less, answering exactly what they asked.
   - Send the calendar invite yourself for a specific slot in their time zone.
   - Never a one-liner, never an essay, never argue with a decline.
   - Pending decision: when a prospect explicitly asks for prices, send a one-page summary with the range. This needs my OK first.
10. **Sending:**
    - 20 to 40 new emails a day, weekdays only.
    - 8 to 11am in the recipient's time zone, with Gmail schedule-send.
    - A few minutes between emails.
    - Never re-mail a bounced address.
    - Pause India sends in Diwali week (6 to 11 Nov).
    - Log every send in the tracker the same day.
